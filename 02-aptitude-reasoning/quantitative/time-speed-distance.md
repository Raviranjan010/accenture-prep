# Quantitative Aptitude: Time, Speed & Distance

## What this is
Time, Speed, and Distance (TSD) evaluates proportional motion dynamics across linear tracks, train passing problems, relative velocity scenarios, circular tracks, and boat-stream currents. In Accenture quantitative tests, speed conversion, train length calculations, and relative motion are high-frequency test items.

---

## Formula / Rule / Pattern

| Concept | Primary Formula | Application Context |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Fundamental Relation** | $\text{Distance} = \text{Speed} \times \text{Time}$ | Basic kinematics |
| **Unit Conversion (km/h $\rightarrow$ m/s)** | $\text{Speed in m/s} = \text{Speed in km/h} \times \frac{5}{18}$ | Metric unit alignment |
| **Unit Conversion (m/s $\rightarrow$ km/h)** | $\text{Speed in km/h} = \text{Speed in m/s} \times \frac{18}{5}$ | Metric unit alignment |
| **Average Speed (Equal Distances)** | $\text{Avg Speed} = \frac{2xy}{x+y}$ | Equal distance out & back |
| **Relative Speed (Opposite Direction)** | $S_{\text{rel}} = S_1 + S_2$ | Objects moving towards each other |
| **Relative Speed (Same Direction)** | $S_{\text{rel}} = |S_1 - S_2|$ | Overtaking scenarios |

## Shortcuts & Tricks

### Shortcut 1: The 5/18 Reflex Conversion
> [!TIP]
> Convert km/h to m/s by multiplying by $\frac{5}{18}$ as an **instant first step** before setting up train or distance equations.
> 
> *Why it works*: $1 \text{ km/h} = \frac{1000 \text{ meters}}{3600 \text{ seconds}} = \frac{5}{18} \text{ m/s}$. Executing this conversion immediately prevents dimensional mismatch errors when train length is given in meters and time in seconds.

### Shortcut 2: Equal Distance Average Speed Formula
> [!TIP]
> If a trip covers distance $D$ at speed $x$ and returns over the same distance $D$ at speed $y$, the average speed is strictly $\frac{2xy}{x+y}$. Do NOT compute the arithmetic mean $\frac{x+y}{2}$.
> 
> *Why it works*: Average speed is $\frac{\text{Total Distance}}{\text{Total Time}} = \frac{2D}{\frac{D}{x} + \frac{D}{y}} = \frac{2xy}{x+y}$.

## Worked Examples

### Example 1: Train Passing Stationary Pole (Easy)
- **Question**: A 180m long train travels at 72 km/h. How many seconds does it take to pass a telegraph pole?
- **Step-by-step Solution**:
  1. Convert speed to m/s: $72 \times \frac{5}{18} = 20 \text{ m/s}$.
  2. Distance to cover $= \text{Train length} = 180 \text{ m}$.
  3. Time $= \frac{180}{20} = 9 \text{ seconds}$.

### Example 2: Train Passing Moving Platform (Medium)
- **Question**: A train 200m long moving at 54 km/h passes a platform 100m long. How long does the train take to completely cross the platform?
- **Step-by-step Solution**:
  1. Convert speed: $54 \times \frac{5}{18} = 15 \text{ m/s}$.
  2. Total distance $= \text{Train length} + \text{Platform length} = 200 + 100 = 300 \text{ m}$.
  3. Time $= \frac{300}{15} = 20 \text{ seconds}$.

### Example 3: Relative Speed Overtaking (Hard)
- **Question**: Two trains 120m and 80m long run in opposite directions on parallel tracks at 42 km/h and 30 km/h respectively. How long will they take to cross each other?
- **Step-by-step Solution**:
  1. Total distance $= 120 + 80 = 200 \text{ m}$.
  2. Relative speed (opposite directions) $= 42 + 30 = 72 \text{ km/h}$.
  3. Convert relative speed: $72 \times \frac{5}{18} = 20 \text{ m/s}$.
  4. Time $= \frac{200}{20} = 10 \text{ seconds}$.

Q1. Convert 90 km/h into m/s.  
a) 20 m/s  
b) 25 m/s  
c) 30 m/s  
d) 15 m/s  

Q2. A man travels at 40 km/h for 2 hours and at 60 km/h for 3 hours. What is his overall average speed?  
a) 50 km/h  
b) 52 km/h  
c) 48 km/h  
d) 54 km/h  

Q3. A car covers a certain distance at 60 km/h and returns at 40 km/h. What is the average speed for the round trip?  
a) 50 km/h  
b) 48 km/h  
c) 45 km/h  
d) 52 km/h

a) 108 km/h  
b) 90 km/h  
c) 72 km/h  
d) 120 km/h

## Answers

1. **b) 25 m/s** — $90 \times 5/18 = 25$ m/s.
2. **b) 52 km/h** — $\text{Total D} = (40 \times 2) + (60 \times 3) = 80 + 180 = 260$ km. $\text{Total T} = 5$ h. Avg $= 260/5 = 52$ km/h.
3. **b) 48 km/h** — $\frac{2(60)(40)}{60+40} = \frac{4800}{100} = 48$ km/h.
4. **a) 108 km/h** — Speed $= 150/5 = 30$ m/s $= 30 \times 18/5 = 108$ km/h.
5. **b) 3 hours** — $S_{\text{rel}} = 60+40 = 100$ km/h. Time $= 300/100 = 3$ hours.
6. **b) 60 minutes** — Speed ratio $= 3:4 \implies$ Time ratio $= 4:3$. Difference $= 1$ part $= 20$ min. Usual time ($3$ parts) $= 60$ minutes.
7. **a) 15 km/h** — Still water speed $= (18+12)/2 = 15$ km/h.
8. **a) 3 km/h** — Stream speed $= (18-12)/2 = 3$ km/h.
9. **a) 14.4 seconds** — $\frac{2 t_1 t_2}{t_1 + t_2} = \frac{2(18)(12)}{18+12} = \frac{432}{30} = 14.4$ seconds.
10. **b) 5 PM** — At 2 PM, thief lead $= 45$ km. $S_{\text{rel}} = 60 - 45 = 15$ km/h. Time to catch $= 45/15 = 3$ hours $\implies 2 \text{ PM} + 3 \text{ h} = 5 \text{ PM}$.
11. **a) 8 km** — Downstream $S = 6+2=8$. Upstream $S = 6-2=4$. Distance $D: \frac{D}{8} + \frac{D}{4} = 3 \implies \frac{3D}{8} = 3 \implies D = 8$ km.
12. **a) 50 km** — Let time be $T$. $14T - 10T = 20 \implies 4T = 20 \implies T = 5$ hours. Actual distance $= 10 \times 5 = 50$ km.
13. **a) 7.33 seconds** — $S_{\text{rel}} = 60 - 6 = 54$ km/h $= 54 \times 5/18 = 15$ m/s. Time $= 110/15 = 7.33$ seconds.
14. **b) 87.5 km/h** — Speed 2 $= 400/4 = 100$ km/h ($8$ parts). $1 \text{ part} = 12.5$. Speed 1 ($7$ parts) $= 7 \times 12.5 = 87.5$ km/h.
15. **a) The rate of separation or closure is the sum of their individual displacements...** — Physical vector addition of relative velocities.

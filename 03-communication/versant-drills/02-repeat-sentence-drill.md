# Versant Communication Assessment: Section 2 — Repeat Sentence Drill

## What this is
Section 2 of the Accenture Communication Assessment plays **16 short-to-medium audio sentences** (~5 to 12 words long). Immediately after hearing each audio prompt, a beep sounds and you must repeat the sentence **verbatim** into your headset microphone within 10 seconds.

---

## Formula / Rule / Pattern

```
[Audio Input] ──> Extract (Subject + Action Verb + Object/Detail) ──> [Spoken Output]
```

- **Audio Playback**: Played ONCE only. No repeat playback allowed.
- **Scoring Focus**: Word order accuracy, syllable stress, listening recall, and unbroken fluency.

---

## Shortcut: The Sentence Shape Extraction Technique

> [!TIP]
> ### Sentence Shape Extraction Trick
> Don't try to memorize the sentence word-for-word in your head while still listening. Instead, extract the **sentence's conceptual "shape"**—`(Subject) + (Verb) + (Key Noun/Location)`—as you hear it, and reconstruct the sentence immediately upon hearing the beep.
> 
> *Why it works*: Rote verbatim memory fails beyond 7–8 words under time pressure, but structural reconstruction scales effortlessly to 12–15 word sentences.

## Practice Questions & Guidelines

Q1. How many total sentences are played in the Repeat Sentence section of Versant?  
a) 8  
b) 16  
c) 24  
d) 10  

Q2. What is the most effective memory strategy when listening to a long 12-word sentence?  
a) Trying to write down every word on paper  
b) Extracting the structural shape (Subject-Verb-Object) and reconstructing  
c) Repeating the first word 10 times  
d) Closing your eyes and guessing  

Q3. What happens if you miss one small pre-position (e.g. "to" or "in") while repeating?  
a) You still receive partial credit for correct word order and main content words  
b) Score drops to zero  
c) Test terminates  
d) The prompt repeats  

---

## Answers

1. **b) 16** — Standard Versant test pattern.
2. **b) Extracting the structural shape (Subject-Verb-Object) and reconstructing** — Structural recall trick.
3. **a) You still receive partial credit...** — Versant scoring evaluates phrase coverage and content words.
